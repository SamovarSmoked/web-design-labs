Sparse Attention Architectures Cut Million-Token Context Latency by 68%

By Dr. Elena Vance · Published in ArXiv AI Digest · Nov 07, 2026 · 6 min read
Open Bookmarks (3)

PulseAI Executive Summary (TL;DR)

  • Reduces KV-cache memory footprint by 3.4x on 1M-token sequences without perplexity degradation.
  • Achieves 68% lower time-to-first-token (TTFT) on standard H200 and B200 GPU clusters.
  • Open-weight reference kernels released in CUDA and Triton under Apache 2.0 license.

1. Overcoming the Quadratic Attention Bottleneck

Long-context reasoning across massive codebases and multi-document repositories has remained bottlenecked by the quadratic scaling of standard self-attention and the linear growth of Key-Value (KV) cache states in GPU high-bandwidth memory.

The newly proposed Dynamic Block-Sparse Routing (DBSR) architecture partitions context windows into semantic summary landmarks. Instead of attending to every historical token at every layer, upper transformer blocks query a compressed router graph that activates only the top-k relevant memory blocks.

2. Empirical Benchmark Evaluation

Benchmarks across Needle-in-a-Haystack, RepoBench-2026, and LongBench demonstrate 99.2% accuracy retention compared to dense attention while increasing throughput by 2.9x on single-node servers.
